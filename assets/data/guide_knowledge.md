# School Accessibility Companion Guide - condensed

Source: Ministry of Education and Sports (MOES), Department of Special Needs and
Inclusive Education, "School Accessibility Companion Guide to the 2019 Building
Control Code". Page numbers below are the printed page numbers in the guide.
Front matter pages (I, II, III) come before page 1.

References in brackets are the ones the guide gives:
- "Part I / II / III / IV" = the Building Control (Accessibility Standards for
  Persons with Disabilities) Code, 2019 (Statutory Instrument 2019 No. 52).
- "MOES recommendation" = advice from the Ministry, not a legal requirement.
- "CMU" = MOES Construction Management Unit standard drawings.
- "ISO 21542" = the international standard on accessibility of buildings.

Some checklist rows in the guide are only partly readable in the text version;
those are left out here rather than guessed.

## About the guide (Guide pp. I-III)

### Why the guide exists (Guide p. II)
- Children with disabilities face more difficulty in accessing schools than
  children without disabilities (UBOS, 2019). The school attendance rate of
  persons with disabilities aged 6-24 was 61.3%, against 68.1% for persons
  without disabilities.
- One of the major barriers is inaccessible school compounds, buildings and
  facilities.
- Many schools remain inaccessible because:
  - many buildings built before 2019 are not refurbished, due to lack of funds
    and planning;
  - new buildings after 2019 do not comply with the Building Control Code,
    because the Code is not yet widely known by architects and civil engineers
    and Building Committees are not fully established in all districts.
- The guide is a simple, friendly companion to the 2019 Building Control Code,
  specific to school environments, to stop further construction of
  inaccessible schools.

### Laws and policies it follows (Guide pp. II-III)
- The Building Control Act, 2013. Each district should have a Building
  Committee that must "ensure that the design and construction of buildings and
  utilities to which the public is to have access cater for persons with
  disabilities" (Art 29.c).
- Statutory Instrument 2019 (No 52), the Building Control (Accessibility
  Standards for Persons with Disabilities) Code.
- Persons with Disabilities Act 2020, Article 6 (non-discrimination in
  institutions of learning). Clause 4 (b): institutions of learning that enrol
  a learner with disability should make the necessary structural adjustments to
  enable access to the buildings or premises.
- Earlier reference: the 2010 Accessibility Standards: a practical guide to
  create a barrier-free physical environment in Uganda (Ministry of Gender,
  Labour and Social Development and UNAPD).
- The guide supports Sustainable Development Goal 4 (inclusive education) and
  the Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (Article 9 on
  Accessibility and Article 24 on Education).

### Who should use it (Guide p. III)
- Architects and civil engineers approving and executing new school
  construction or refurbishment of existing schools.
- Education actors managing school construction or refurbishment projects.
- Users of schools: students, teachers, parents and community members.

### How to use it (Guide p. III)
Purpose: to assess the current situation, or to evaluate construction or
refurbishment work that is ongoing or completed.
1. Set up and train a team including students, teachers, parents, community
   members, etc. Include persons with different disabilities.
2. Prepare the guide, a pen, a measure and a camera.
3. Following the guide, visit the different facilities in the school.
4. Read the standards and answer YES or NO. If needed, take measurements to
   decide if the current status meets the standards.
5. Write a report of results. Prioritise inaccessible elements that require
   improvement, and develop and implement action plans (for example the School
   Improvement Plan).
6. Repeat the assessment regularly for continuous improvement.

Other notes (Guide p. III):
- MOES has standard drawings for key school facilities (3-classroom block for
  primary, 2-classroom block for secondary, latrine block). MOES is revising
  them to comply with the 2019 Building Control Code. Contact the Construction
  Management Unit for more information.
- The guide comes with a training pack (a PowerPoint file).

## 01 School compound (Guide pp. 1-4)

### Signs (Guide p. 2)
- There is a sign showing the direction and name of the facilities in the
  school compound, at the gate (minimum) and in other common places
  (recommended: main hall). (Part II 4 (2))
- A sign has lettering of height not less than 50mm. (Part II 4 (2))
- Directional signage is mounted (protruding or on a wall), and the
  information is big and readable from a distance. (Part II 8 (2); ISO 21542
  and a suggestion by the association of little people)

### Accessible route (Guide pp. 1-2)
- There is at least one accessible route that connects the facilities in the
  school compound. (Part II 6)
- The clear width of the walking surface is not less than 1.2m.
  (Part II 6 (4-a))
- There is a pause area (with seating) for every 25m. (Part II 6 (7))
- The surface is slip-resistant: stable, flat, firm and anti-slip.
  (Part II 9 (2)(10))
- The accessible route has a clear turning space of minimum 1.5m diameter.
  (Part II 7 (1))
- A door or window does not swing into, and no object protrudes into, an
  accessible walkway, corridor, turning space, stair or ramp.
  (Part II 7 (2) / 8 (3))
- The compound drawing (p. 1) shows accessible pathways over 1.2m wide linking
  the gate, classroom blocks, toilets, kitchen and playground, with 1.5m
  diameter turning spaces.
- The drawing on p. 3 shows a suggestion box placed 0.9-1.0m above the ground.

### Doorstops, drains and surfaces (Guide p. 4)
- A doorstop is positioned so that a door opens to its maximum and does not
  create a hazard for people passing the walkway. (Part II 8 (4))
- A drainage grating that crosses an accessible path is placed perpendicular to
  the main walking direction, and the gap between grating and path does not
  exceed 13mm. (Part II 8 (7-a), 8 (7-b))
- Openings: if circular, less than 13mm diameter; if elongated, less than 13mm
  wide and perpendicular to the dominant direction of travel.
  (Part II 9 (7-a), 9 (7-b))
- Where a surface is cambered for drainage, the camber does not exceed 1:50.
  (Part II 9 (9))

### Room signs (Guide pp. 7-8)
- All rooms show the grade number (P1, etc.) or the room's function (for
  example library, laboratory) on a wall beside the door frame.
  (MOES recommendation)
- Signs are engraved or brailled. (Part II 10 (13))
- Signs have contrasting colours and large print, readable from a distance.
  (Part II 10 (13))
- Signage is mounted at least 2.0m above the trafficable surface.
  (Part II 8 (2))
- Signage is also mounted at 0.5m above the trafficable surface for persons
  with short stature, without protruding from the wall. (ISO 21542, suggestion
  by the association of little people)

### Doors (Guide pp. 7-8)
- Clear opening of the door at least 0.90m (primary) and 1.2m (secondary,
  two-leaf door 0.2m + 1.0m). (Part II 10 (2) and CMU, MOES)
- Space for a wheelchair user to make a 90 degree turn (turning space 1.5 x
  1.5m). (Part II 10 (4))
- A door is light and easy to open for everyone. (Part II 10 (9))
- A doorstop keeps a door open to its maximum and does not create a hazard.
  (Part II 8 (4))
- A glass door has a horizontal marking in a contrasting, non-glaring colour at
  1.25m-1.60m from the floor to warn persons with visual impairment.
  (Part II 10 (11))
- Door and door frame are painted in a colour that contrasts with the
  surrounding wall. (Part II 10 (12))
- Door has kick plates of 0.30-0.40m from the bottom of the door.
  (MOES recommendation)
- Door handle is a lever type, easily usable with one hand, not a round door
  knob. (Part II 11 (1)(2))
- Door handle is fitted at a height not exceeding 1.0m from the floor.
  (Part II 11 (1))

## 03 Library (Guide pp. 9-10)
A library may use an empty classroom, so the classroom block standards also
apply.
- Windows (glazed) 1.2m wide x 1.5m high. (CMU, MOES, Sep 2020)
- Clear width of veranda in front of the library: min. 1.5m. (CMU, MOES)
- Door width at least 0.9m in primary and 1.2m in secondary (two-leaf door
  0.2m + 1.0m). (Part II 10 (2) and CMU, MOES)
- Enough lighting without causing glare or shadows. (Part IV 49 (2, 4))
- Books in Braille, large font, audio, etc. are available.
  (MOES recommendation)
- Detached desks and seats, and/or height-adjustable desks and chairs, are
  available. (MOES recommendation)
- Directional signage is mounted (protruding or on a wall), and the
  information is big and readable from a distance. (Part II 8 (2))
- The drawing (p. 9) shows aisles over 1.2m, height-adjusted furniture, Braille
  books and information in large font.

## 04 Accessible ramp and stairway (Guide pp. 11-14, 31)

### Ramps (Guide p. 12)
- For any change in level over 25mm, there is a ramp. (Part II 9 (8), 12)
  - with a gradient of 1:20, and not steeper than 1:12
    (Part I 3 interpretation, Part II 12 (3-a));
  - with a landing space of 1.5m x 1.1m at the top and bottom of each ramp
    (Part II 12 (3-d));
  - with a handrail on both sides (Part II 12 (3-e)).
- Long ramps have intermediate landings: every 10m for a 1:20 slope, every
  2.26m for a 1:12 slope. (ISO 21542 (2021))
- A landing of a ramp on the accessible route does not overlap a landing for
  another ramp. (Part II 12 (4))
- A door leaf or window does not open onto a ramp or landing space.
  (Part II 12 (3-d, 5))
- The drawing (p. 11) shows a door width of min. 0.9m, kick plates of
  0.3-0.4m, landings of 1.5m and over 1.1m, and a dimension marked "at least
  0.9m".

### Checking a ramp's slope (Guide p. 31)
- Ideal gradient 1:20. Maximum gradient 1:12. Steeper than 1:12 (for example
  1:10 or 1:8) is not acceptable.
- Engineers need to consider space and topography on the site to design the
  ramp orientation and achieve a 1:20 slope.
- Slope, maximum length between landings and maximum rise:
  | Slope | % | Max length between landings | Max rise |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1:20 | 5% | 10m | 0.50m |
  | 1:16 | 6.25% | 6m | 0.375m |
  | 1:14 | 7.14% | 3.5m | 0.25m |
  | 1:12 | 8.33% | 2.26m | 0.188m |
  | 1:10 | 10% | not acceptable | |
  | 1:8 | 12.5% | not acceptable | |
- A slope such as 1:20 is a ratio of rise to run, not the actual length.
- How to calculate the slope:
  1. Measure the height (a) and the length along the bottom of the ramp (b),
     in the same unit.
  2. Divide the length by the height (b / a).
  3. If the answer is 20 or higher, the gradient is ok. If it is less than 12,
     the ramp is too steep, does not meet the standards and should be
     re-designed.
  Examples: 10.0m / 0.50m = 20 (ok). 3.0m / 0.75m = 4 (less than 12, too
  steep).

### Stairways (Guide pp. 13-14)
- A stairway has a width of at least 0.9m. (Part II 13 (1-a))
- Each step rise is the same height and below 0.17m. (Part II 13 (1-c))
- Each nosing has a contrasting colour, 0.04 x 0.04m. (Part II 13 (2))
- Water should not accumulate on a walking surface, including treads.
- A tactile marking with contrasting colours at the top and bottom of the
  stairs. (Part II 13 (4))
- No winders or spiral stairs; use proper landing spaces instead.
  (Part II 13 (1-g/h))

### Handrails and guardrails (Guide p. 14)
- A handrail or guardrail does not encroach into the accessible pathway.
  (Part II 14 (1-a))
- Elliptical grip 50 x 40mm; if circular, diameter between 30-50mm.
  (Part II 14 (1-b))
- Height to the top of the rail: 0.6-0.7m for the lower one and 0.8-0.9m for
  the upper one. (Instruction from MOWT)
- A handrail or guardrail extends 0.3m horizontally beyond the top and bottom
  of a ramp or stairway. (Part II 14 (1-i-ii))
- The height of the rail is consistent along its length. (Part II 14 (1-c))

## 05 Bathroom block (Guide pp. 15-24)

### Accessible toilets (Guide p. 16)
- Each school washroom facility has at least two toilets (male/female) or at
  least one unisex toilet accessible to students with disabilities.
  (Part III 16 (1))
- The accessible toilet is as close as possible to the classroom (15m, MOES
  recommendation) or at an equal distance to that of persons without
  disabilities. Maximum distance 45m. (Part III 16 (1d))
- The exterior door has a minimum width of 0.9m when fully open.
  (Part III 16 (2))
- The door of the accessible toilet:
  - opens outward (Part III 17 (1-a-i));
  - has an easy-to-use lock operated with one hand, not a latch
    (Part III 17 (1-a-iii));
  - can be opened from the outside with a suitable device in an emergency, and
    shows whether the toilet is occupied (Part III 17 (1-a-iv));
  - runs smoothly, with a handle or grab rail on either side that is easy to
    operate (Part III 37 (1));
  - the distance between the handle and the door surface is 50mm
    (Part III 37 (2)).

### Toilet compartment (Guide pp. 17-18)
- Wall-to-wall size of the compartment is minimum 1.9m x 2.3m.
  (Part III 17 (2))
- A turning diameter of 1.5m for wheelchair users, free of obstructions.
  (Part III 17 (4))
- Toilet seat height 0.48m-0.50m. (Part III 17 (5))
- The toilet seat is a full round type, not open fronted, with minimum contours
  on the top surface. (Part III 18 (1a))
- The seat and lid stay upright when raised. (Part III 18 (2))
- Where a backrest is provided, it is 0.15-0.20m high and 0.35-0.40m wide.
  (Part III 19 (1-c))

### Washbasins and taps (Guide pp. 19-20)
- The washbasin is mounted without legs or pedestal; the drawing shows its
  height below 0.82m. (Part III 22 (a))
- The washbasin has a vertical clearance of 0.65m underneath.
  (Part III 22 (b))
- Taps have lever handles, sensor plates or similar controls
  (Part III 23 (a)), with at least 50mm clearance from an adjacent surface
  (Part III 23 (b)). Where there are separate hot and cold taps, the hot tap is
  on the left (Part III 23 (c)).
- The vanity slab is at least 0.12m wide beside the basin. (Part III 24 (b))

### Grab rails (Guide pp. 19-20)
- A grab rail is at the side and back of the toilet. (Part III 25 (1))
- A hinged support arm is also provided on the transfer side. (Part III 26)
- Grab rail outside diameter between 30-40mm. (Part III 25 (2-a))
- Exposed edges and corners of grab rails have a radius of at least 5mm.
  (Part III 25 (2-b))
- The hand can pass along the full length of a vertical grab rail without
  obstruction. (Part III 25 (4))

### Bathrooms and showers (Guide pp. 21-22)
- There is a bathroom of at least 1.0 x 2.0m so a wheelchair user can enter
  with ease. (Part III 30 (1))
- The bathroom door opening is at least 0.90m. (Part III 30 (3))
- The pathway to the bathroom is gentle, non-slippery and connected with a ramp
  where necessary. (Part III 30 (5))
- Directional signs to the bathroom in contrasting colours, with engraved
  letters or Braille. (Part III 30 (6))
- A hook or peg (on a door or wall) is mounted at 1.1-1.6m.
  (Part III 27 (1)(2), 32 (1)(2))
- The shower cubicle has no kerb or change of level, so a wheelchair user can
  roll in. (Part III 29 (1))
- The drawing (p. 21) shows a shower seat at 0.45-0.50m.

### Urinals (Guide p. 24)
- The urinal has a designated area for a person with disability, wide enough
  for a wheelchair user. (Part III 33 (1))
- The urinal is stall-type or wall-hung with an elongated rim.
  (Part III 33 (2))
- It allows a forward approach by a wheelchair user. (Part III 33 (4))
- Where needed, a ramp to the urinals is built with non-slippery materials.
  (Part III 33 (5))

## 06 Pit latrine (Guide pp. 24-26)
- The pit latrine room is at least 2.1m x 1.8m. (Part III 34 (1))
- A turning radius for a wheelchair user of 1.5m, free of obstruction.
  (Part III 34 (2))
- The pathway to the latrine entrance has no changes in level. Where a change
  of level exists, it is connected with a horizontal landing area of 1.7 x
  1.5m. (Part III 35 (1)(2)(3))
- Clear door opening of 1.2m (MOES recommendation, a two-leaf door 0.2m +
  1.0m) and not less than 0.9m. (Part III 36 (3))
- A smooth running door, with a handle and lock that are easy to operate and
  grasp with one hand. (Part III 37 (1))
- The distance between the handle and the door surface is at least 50mm, with
  a pull-back handrail inside the door. (Part III 37 (2))
- The door opens outwards. (Part III 37 (3))
- Double handrails for support on each side. (Part III 38 (1)) The drawing
  shows handrails at 0.6-0.7m and 0.8-0.9m.
- Handrails help a person with disability walk from the door to the latrine,
  and a handrail on the outside wall gives support while opening the door.
  (Part III 38 (2))
- The latrine seat is made of cemented plastered bricks or concrete, with no
  sharp edges. (Part III 39 (2))
- The seat is painted with waterproof paint for easy cleaning, and the paint is
  regularly maintained. (Part III 39 (3))
- The universally accepted symbol for accessibility is displayed outside.
  (Part III 40)
- Corridors inside the pit latrine room are not less than 1.5m. (Part III 41)
- A hole high up on the wall gives ventilation and light. (Part III 42)
- The surface slopes towards the latrine hole from all corners, so splashing
  water, urine and refuse go to the hole. (Part III 43; the start of this row is
  cut off in the text version)

## Annex: Gaps in school construction and recommendations (Guide pp. 32-33)
Throughout: refer to the 2019 Building Control Code during planning and
implementation, by both physical planning and building committees.

- Needs assessment (site survey). Gap: not participatory with students,
  teachers, the District Education Office, etc., so accessibility problems are
  missed; results not validated by school personnel. Recommendation: schools
  conduct a participatory assessment, involving key actors. (p. 32)
- Design (drawings and Bill of Quantities). Gap: MOES standard designs were
  made before the 2019 Code and are not updated; they do not fit every school's
  topography and are not adapted; poor coordination between physical planning
  and building committees; inappropriate drawings and BOQs lead to a lack of
  funds that leaves out accessibility. Recommendations: MOES updates the
  standard drawings; use qualified engineers; MOES, Ministry of Local
  Government and District Local Government promote customising the drawings to
  real environments and needs. (p. 32)
- Approval. Gap: Building Committees not established or not functional; they
  approve designs without confirming compliance with the 2019 Code; approval
  happens only once a month. Recommendations: the National Building Review
  Board instructs all districts to establish Building Committees; MOES, MOWT,
  Ministry of Local Government and districts train all committee members on the
  Code; Local Government speeds up approvals. (pp. 32-33)
- BOQ shared with district engineers. Gap: compliance not checked.
  Recommendation: share the unpriced BOQ with all required partners (OPM, MOES,
  DEO, LC, CAO, RWC, UNHCR, etc.). (p. 33)
- Procurement of engineers and materials. Gap: compliance not checked; lack of
  funds due to poor planning and approvals. Recommendation: MOES, District
  Local Government and Ministry of Local Government ensure compliance. (p. 33)
- During construction. Gap: inspection and monitoring do not involve key
  actors and do not follow the Code. Recommendations: qualified engineers
  supervise; Local Government Planning Unit, MOES (construction and planning
  unit, SN/IE Department), OPM and UNHCR take part in technical inspection;
  participatory joint monitoring. (p. 33)
- Occupation permit and handover. Gap: inspection not in line with the Code;
  fees expensive or inconsistent; slow; compliance not checked at handover.
  Recommendations: involve District Local Government in the inspection;
  standardise fees (government projects should not be charged); partners engage
  government at the start of construction plans. (p. 33)
